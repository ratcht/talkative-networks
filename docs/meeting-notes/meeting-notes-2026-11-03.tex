\section{New Testbed}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Supports both MNIST and Fashion-MNIST.
  \item Expert generation is completely modularised and independent of joint training.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item This allows us to enforce diversity through several methods, such as class skew,
            negative correlation learning and seed variation, without affecting the training
            of the communication channel.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Experts are generated per trial with a different random seed; this means our
        architectural decisions don't fit a specific collection of experts.
  \item The testbed supports several selection strategies for the training script to use: we can
        select a random subset of the trial's pool, or sort on some criterion and pick the top $k$.
  \item The communication channel has been further modularised.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item The mixing policy (attention, cosine attention, mean routing, self routing, etc.) can
            be specified independently.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Encoder and decoder objects no longer make any spatial assumptions about their input.
  \item We now use MLflow for experiment tracking instead of Aim; seeds are paired across arms.
  \item Supports forwards and backwards communication configurations.
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================= baseline
\section{Experimentation: Baseline Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Generate a fresh pool of 3 experts for each trial seed using class skew
        (Dirichlet $\alpha = 1$), and train:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item each expert on 50\% of Fashion-MNIST for 10 epochs;
      \item then freeze the experts, and train whatever communication architecture sits between
            them as an ensemble on a separate 40\%.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Every arm trains for 30 epochs at learning rate $10^{-3}$ on the ensemble's averaged
        prediction.
  \item We compare the performance of:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Communicative Ensemble: Linear}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \emph{Encoder:} feature map at the read port \ra{} linear \ra{} $r = 8$ \ra{}
                Q (16), K (16), V (64)
          \item \emph{Mixer:} attention, single head, over every expert's message including its own
          \item \emph{Decoder:} received message (64) \ra{} linear \ra{} $r = 8$ \ra{} linear to
                the full feature map at the write port
        \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Communicative Ensemble: Non-linear.} Same as Linear, with a GELU on the
            $r = 8$ taper of the encoder, the decoder, or both (three variants).
      \item \textbf{Self-Routing: Linear / Non-linear}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Same as the Communicative Ensemble, but each expert receives only its own message
                (Q and K are unused).
        \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Mean-Routing: Linear / Non-linear}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Same as the Communicative Ensemble, but each expert receives the plain average of
                every expert's message, its own included (Q and K unused).
        \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Dense Soft-Gated Mixture of Experts}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \emph{Gate:} each expert's feature map at the read port \ra{} linear \ra{} $r = 8$
                \ra{} concatenate ($3 \times 8$) \ra{} GELU \ra{} linear \ra{} 3 scores \ra{}
                softmax \ra{} weights on the experts' answer probabilities
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
  \item Channels are initialised to start non-communicative, at bagging.
  \item We use bagging as the combiner rule: the frozen experts' averaged probabilities, which
        every arm starts from.
  \item All experts stay frozen; only the channel or gate trains.
  \item Each run is scored as the median test accuracy over the 5 epochs around its best
        \texttt{joint\_val} epoch, averaged across trial seeds.
  \item The study is split into 4 phases:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Phase 1} determines the read and write ports of both the forwards and backwards
            communication channels, and the read port of the MoE.
        \begin{itemize}
          \item This is done using linear channels and their Self-Routing control at all 6 port
                pairs, and the MoE at all 3 read ports.
          \item Picked:
            \begin{itemize}
              \item Forwards: stage2 \ra{} stage3
              \item Backwards: stage3 \ra{} stage1
              \item MoE: reads at stage3
            \end{itemize}
        \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Phase 2a} introduces non-linearities (GELU in the encoder, the decoder, or both)
            into an otherwise linear forwards communication channel, and compares to Self-Routing.
      \item \textbf{Phase 2b} introduces the same non-linearities into an otherwise linear
            backwards communication channel, and compares to Self-Routing.
      \item \textbf{Phase 2c} compares against Mean-Routing at all channel non-linearity levels,
            forwards and backwards.
      \item \textbf{Phase 3} sweeps the taper dimension $r$ of the encoders and decoders at
            $r = 16$, 24 and 32 against the $r = 8$ baseline, using the linear and GELU channels
            with attention, Self- and Mean-Routing, forwards and backwards.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================= degenerate cases
\section{Experimentation: Degenerate Cases}

\subsection{Setup}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Holds the channel fixed and changes the expert group instead. The channel is the Linear
        channel at $r = 8$, at the ports picked in Phase 1, with Self-, Mean- and
        Attention-Routing, forwards and backwards, on the same 15 trial seeds.
  \item Four groups:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Clones:} three copies of the trial's first expert (30 epochs).
      \item \textbf{Two Trained + One Untrained:} the trial's pool with its third expert left at
            random weights (60 epochs).
      \item \textbf{Negative Correlation:} three experts trained together to disagree, at strength
            $\lambda = 0$, 2.5 and 4, without class skew (30 epochs). The MoE is also run at each
            $\lambda$. $\lambda = 4$ was picked after the $\lambda$ sweep had been read.
      \item \textbf{Untrained:} three experts with random weights (150 epochs).
    \end{itemize}
  \item Each expert's channel starts from its own random initialisation, so clones are identical
        only until training starts.
  \item Same scoring as the baseline. Every result below also holds on validation images at fixed
        epochs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Results}

\finding[24]{Every Group Converges to the Same Kind of Expert}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Whatever the group starts as, training leaves its experts about 88\% accurate and agreeing
        88--93\% of the time. Copies drift apart to that point; experts trained to disagree come
        back to it within the first epoch.
  \item Agreement beyond what accuracy alone explains (shared mistakes) also ends at the same level,
        about where the baseline pool already starts.
  \item The channel does most of this on its own: Self-Routing gets there too. Hearing others adds
        a small push on top: Mean-Routing's agreement beyond accuracy is 0.6--1.3 points above
        Self-Routing's in every group of trained experts.
\end{itemize}

\finding[22]{Clones: Each Expert's Own Channel Makes the Copies Different}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Before training the copies give identical answers, so at least one is right only as often
        as one expert. After training, at least one copy is right on 94--95\% of images, nearly as
        many as three separately trained experts cover. Self-Routing does this too, so the
        diversity comes from each copy's own channel, not from communication.
  \item Mean-Routing pulls the copies back together. Mean $-$ Self, forwards / backwards:
        individual accuracy \pmse{+0.71}{0.15}{15} / \pmse{+0.52}{0.15}{15}; agreement
        \pmse{+2.40}{0.42}{15} / \pmse{+2.08}{0.37}{15}; at least one expert right
        \pmse{-0.65}{0.11}{0} / \pmse{-0.76}{0.16}{0}. Ensemble accuracy doesn't move
        (\pmse{+0.03}{0.08}{7} / \pmse{-0.06}{0.10}{5}).
\end{itemize}

\finding[18]{Two Trained + One Untrained: The Untrained Expert Learns What the Others Already
Know}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Listening lifts the untrained expert's own accuracy by 6 points forwards and 19 backwards.
        Attention-Routing does no better than Mean-Routing.
  \item It gets there by becoming like the trained experts. Mean $-$ Self, forwards / backwards:
        agreement \pmse{+6.3}{2.4}{15} / \pmse{+29.1}{6.5}{15}; at least one expert right
        \pmse{-0.45}{0.19}{2} / \pmse{-0.59}{0.19}{0}. Its new right answers are on images the
        others already get right.
  \item So the ensemble gains far less: Mean $-$ Self is \pmse{+0.41}{0.13}{14} forwards and
        \pmse{+0.20}{0.12}{13} backwards, and forwards it fades by epoch 60.
\end{itemize}

\finding[24]{Negative Correlation: The Channel Undoes the Disagreement Within One Epoch}

\begin{itemize}
  \item At $\lambda = 4$, each expert alone starts at 39\% and the three almost never agree, yet
        their average is right 89.5\% of the time. After one epoch, every channel, Self-Routing
        included, has turned each expert into a good one on its own, and agreement is already
        81--88\% on validation.
  \item Backwards, Mean-Routing gains on Self-Routing as the experts disagree more: Mean $-$ Self,
        minus the same gap at $\lambda = 0$, is \pmse{+0.15}{0.15}{9} at $\lambda = 2.5$ and
        \pmse{+0.29}{0.16}{12} at $\lambda = 4$ (on validation $+0.19$ and $+0.28$, 15/15). This
        is Self-Routing losing more as $\lambda$ rises, not Mean-Routing gaining. Forwards there is
        no clear gain.
  \item The MoE leaves the experts as they are, and every channel arm beats it at every $\lambda$,
        by 0.33--0.83 points (12--15/15).
\end{itemize}

\finding[24]{Untrained: Attention Helps, Forwards by Making Experts Alike, Backwards by Widening
Coverage}

\begin{itemize}
  \item With nothing to learn from each other, the channel alone still reaches 87\% forwards and
        83\% backwards from random features (Self-Routing, 150 epochs).
  \item By 150 epochs, Attention $-$ Self is \pmse{+0.88}{0.16}{15} forwards and
        \pmse{+1.44}{0.80}{14} backwards; within noise at 30 epochs. Backwards is still climbing,
        so these are lower bounds.
  \item Forwards, attention makes the experts better and more alike (agreement
        \pmse{+12.1}{3.4}{14}, at least one expert right \pmse{-1.57}{0.33}{0}). Backwards, it
        widens what they cover (\pmse{+0.76}{0.30}{13}): the only arm in the study where hearing
        others raises coverage.
  \item Mean-Routing is uneven: by 150 epochs it is ahead of Self on most trials, but 6--13 points
        behind on 3 of 15 backwards.
  \item Unlike forwards elsewhere, routing here varies with the image: the most common routing
        covers 35\% of images forwards and 28\% backwards.
\end{itemize}
